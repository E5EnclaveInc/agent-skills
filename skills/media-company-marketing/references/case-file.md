# Media-Company Case File

Evidence from the source material for the entertainment-first doctrine. Cite these
cases when choosing formats; do not invent others.

## Roomies Roomies Roomies
A rental platform ran a comedic series about NYC apartment hunting with no visible
product branding — pure entertainment about the renter's experience. It earned
millions of views inside its target city. Lesson: the audience comes for the
entertainment; the brand wins by owning the attention, not the logo placement.

## Salt / SULT
A UK electrolyte brand built ~20,000 followers with entertaining content *before
the product existed*. When it launched, it sold out overnight. Lesson: capture
attention first, introduce the product second; the audience is already warmed.

## British Vogue — "intern" content
Around the Millie Bobby Brown cover, social-first "intern" content outperformed
the still image of the cover itself. Lesson: platform-native, personality-led
formats beat polished assets even for prestige brands.

## DoorDash and Currys — deputized employees
Both scaled revenue through employee storytelling on social. Currys' CEO directly
attributed a Q4 revenue increase to employees' TikTok presence. Lesson: if the
founder won't be the face, deputize employees — real people telling the brand's
story beats corporate creative.

## The mechanism
Capture attention where the audience already is. Ride relevant trends. Meet them
via many assets across platforms — clip, algorithm, share — until the purchase
(or the sign-up, or the donation) feels inevitable.
