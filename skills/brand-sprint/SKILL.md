# Brand Sprint

## Purpose
Give a new product or company its identity in one bounded sprint: a name that
survives domain and trademark scrutiny, and a visual register the build can
implement without another meeting. Decisions are scored and adjudicated, never
debated by taste.

## Workflow
1. Set the timebox: one focused pass (the source pattern is 60 minutes; a hard
   multi-day build gets one session). Announce the clock before starting.
2. Generate the field: 15+ candidate names, deliberately varied — descriptive,
   invented, deadline-shaped (the constraint as the brand, e.g. a shipping
   date), and playful. No filtering yet.
3. Score the top 5 against the four checks:
   - Domain obtainable (exact-match preferred; check live, don't assume)
   - Email format clean (`<name>@domain` reads professionally)
   - Works as both company and product brand
   - Trademark/quick-clearance check (search, don't skip)
4. Adjudicate by war-room consensus, not one person's taste. Kill the losers
   in writing with one-line reasons each.
5. Draft the visual direction against the chosen name:
   - Brand voice (the copy register: plain, certain, no hype)
   - Hero copy and CTAs
   - Palette and typography (system stacks preferred; zero new dependencies)
   - Logo directions (mark concept, not a finished file)
   - Implementation-ready design brief a builder can execute alone
6. Provision the identity assets: domain check → register
   decision, GitHub repo, product README. Organizational moves (new org,
   domain purchase) are staged for the human's word, never auto-executed.

## Output Contract
The chosen name with its scorecard and the kill list, the domain/email picks,
the visual register (voice, hero, CTAs, palette, type, logo directions), and
the implementation-ready design brief.

## Operating Rules
1. The clock is announced and kept. A sprint that drifts is a committee.
2. Naming is scored, not voted. Taste is a tiebreaker at most.
3. The kill list is written down — dead names stay dead.
4. The brief must be implementable by one builder alone, no follow-up
   questions. If it needs a meeting, it's not done.
5. Visual direction follows your team's standing design register (or its
   design-protocol document when the site follows one). One expressive gesture
   per composition, never two.
6. No income claims, no hype words, no invented claims about the product —
   the house copy register applies to the sprint's own output too.
