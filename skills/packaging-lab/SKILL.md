# Packaging Lab

## Purpose
Apply the packaging discipline of a world-class content operation: pre-test
packaging before publishing, lead every edit with the retention spikes, run
outreach as a numbers game, and hold publishing slots and a ratcheting quality bar.

## Workflow
1. Pre-test packaging before publishing. Produce multiple title variants and
   multiple thumbnail variants per asset; test them before the asset ships (e.g.,
   as ad tests). One title alone can earn ~20% more click-through than another —
   "wouldn't it be a shame if the episode got 20% fewer clicks because we didn't
   get the title right." Ship only the winner. Never publish on the first title.
2. Edit for retention, not narrative. Re-edit to LEAD with the retention spikes:
   open with the action or advice viewers came for, not the backstory ("tell us
   your story" openings get cut).
3. Run outreach as a volume game. Set a weekly outbound target and hit it — the
   operating pattern is 1,000 messages/week → ~10 yeses (from guest booking;
   generalize the volume mindset to any outreach lane: guests, partners,
   affiliates). Sequence outreach strategically so one guest unlocks the next.
4. Hold the slot. Publishing is non-negotiable: it goes out at the set time no
   matter what — the slot is the trust contract with the audience.
5. Ratchet the bar. Standards rise over time, never fall: every release must be
   better than the last. Apply the same bar to people — hire only the absolute
   best, and leave the seat empty rather than lower it.

## Output Contract
Per asset: packaging test results (variants tested, winner, margin over baseline),
retention-edit notes (what leads now), outreach volume log (sent vs target, yeses,
unlock chain), slot compliance, and a one-line bar-ratchet note vs the previous
release.

## Operating Rules
1. No asset ships untested. A single title/thumbnail is a guess; test first.
2. Lead with the spikes. If the opening doesn't carry the strongest moment,
   re-edit.
3. Volume is the outreach strategy. Set the number, hit it weekly, track yeses.
4. The slot never slips. Missed slots break the trust the timetable built.
5. The bar ratchets up. Every release better than the last; never publish or hire
   down to convenience.
