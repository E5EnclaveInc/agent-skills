# Income Blueprint

## Purpose
A repeatable workshop that takes someone from "I don't know how to make money with AI" to
contacting real prospects the same day — without a 90-day plan they'll abandon. Pattern B
(process primitive). Momentum, not plans, is the product.

## When it fires
- Designing a paid workshop/cohort ("your AI income blueprint in a day").
- Validating a new offer before building anything (the Day-One Test).
- Coaching a person or client through idea → first contact.
- NOT for auditing an existing business — that's the audit lane.

## The five steps

### 0. Privacy first (say it out loud)
Confirm the tool's settings don't train on user data before collecting anything.
State it: "your data trains nothing." Trust is the precondition.

### 1. Teach the super prompt (3 moves)
Anyone can prompt well in three moves:
1. **Brain-dump** — dictate what you want, messy, spoken not typed.
2. **"Clean this up for clarity and impact."** — prepend to the dump, run it.
3. **"Now turn this into a super prompt."** — role + context + guardrails. Every major
   tool knows the term; verify by running it.
Add the anti-AI-slop guardrail: ask the tool what marks writing as AI-written, save an
explicit never-use list.

### 2. Personal Intelligence Blueprint (3 passes)
1. Paste the resume: "Read this the way a business advisor would, not a hiring manager.
   Tell me what I'm actually good at based on what I've done, not what I've called it."
2. **"Don't start yet — just tell me you've got it."** Get assignment confirmation
   before it works.
3. Let it interview you to fill everything the resume doesn't say, then write the
   blueprint: who you are, what you're good at, your real constraints, and what kind
   of income opportunity would fit your life. Save it; everything after builds on it.

### 3. Story repository (story bank)
"I want to build a story repository — the personal stories I can use in my marketing, so
it sounds like a person, not a brochure. Based on everything you know about me, ask me
for stories in specific categories (a time I fixed something nobody else could; a moment
I felt stuck...). Ask one at a time, two or three sentences each. Then organize them into
a story bank with a note on what each story is good for."
Maintain monthly: re-interview for new and forgotten stories. Stories are what make the
copy unmistakably theirs.

### 4. Income ideas → pick one
"Using my voice profile, personal intelligence blueprint, and story repository — give me
income ideas that fit ME specifically." Output per idea: why it fits, probability of
earnings in the next 90 days, what it takes, what it demands. Pick one.

### 5. Day-One Test (not a 90-day plan)
"Build me a Day-One Test. Not the 90-day plan. The goal is to find out if anyone wants
this before I build anything. Tell me exactly who to contact today, how to find them
without cold calling, what to say — written in my voice — and what the first offer is:
free, cheap, or paid, and why."
Then convert it into the daily strategist: "You're my business strategist. Tell me what
to do today. I'll report back each day." Daily check-ins, coaching, momentum.

## Operating rules
- Overwhelm kills plans: never deliver the 90-day plan before the Day-One Test lands.
- Every claim about the method is the user's or the workshop's — never invent earnings
  probabilities, testimonials, or client results.
- As a product, this is a workshop: participants leave with a Voice DNA, a blueprint,
  a story bank, three ranked ideas, and a day-one contact list. Charge for the seat,
  certify the completion.
