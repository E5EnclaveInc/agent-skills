# Copy Standards — Pinker clarity, Cialdini persuasion, verified numbers

Every rule is checkable: a gate runner can pass/fail each line of copy against it.

## The Pinker lens (clarity) — from *The Sense of Style*: classic style
- **P-C1 Concrete over abstract.** Prefer the concrete noun and the visible verb.
  *Check: no sentence whose subject is an abstraction doing nothing ("Solutions were leveraged").*
- **P-C2 Kill zombie nouns.** Nominalizations ("utilization", "implementation",
  "operationalization") become verbs. *Check: search the copy for -tion/-ment/-ance
  nouns; each must earn its place or be re-verbed.*
- **P-C3 Coherence: old before new.** Each sentence starts with what the reader
  already knows and ends with the new point. *Check: paragraph topic flow reads
  without re-reading.*
- **P-C4 Show the mechanism.** Don't assert quality ("world-class", "cutting-edge") —
  describe what happens. *Check: every adjective of excellence is backed by a
  mechanism or a number in the same block.*
- **P-C5 Respect the reader's intelligence.** No dumbing down, no hype inflation.
  The approver's audience is changemakers — executive directors, not clicks.
  *Check: the copy would not embarrass the reader for having read it.*

## The Cialdini lens (persuasion) — honest levers only
- **C-C1 Reciprocity.** Give real value before any ask (the playbook excerpt, the
  template, the data). *Check: the page delivers something useful before the CTA.*
- **C-C2 Commitment & consistency.** Small yes before big yes (newsletter → playbook
  → consult). CTAs step up in commitment. *Check: no page asks for the biggest
  commitment first.*
- **C-C3 Social proof.** Real names, real numbers, real outcomes. *Check: every
  testimonial/claim is attributed, dated, and checkable — no anonymous praise, no
  invented statistics.*
- **C-C4 Authority.** Cite the actual source (peer-reviewed paper, filed grant,
  on-chain record). *Check: authority claims name the credentialing body.*
- **C-C5 Liking.** Warmth is real — founder's first-person voice where it belongs
  (video scripts especially). *Check: the voice sounds like a person, not a brand deck.*
- **C-C6 Scarcity.** Only real scarcity (cohort seats, deadline dates). *Check: every
  scarcity claim is falsifiable — a date, a seat count. Manufactured urgency is banned.*
- **C-C7 Unity.** A true shared identity, primed before the ask ("we is the shared me").
  *Check: insider language is the buyer's real vocabulary; the tribe claim would survive
  a buyer audit. "Join the family" from a stranger is banned.*

The deep reference behind C-C1…C-C7 — page patterns, placement, copy templates, the
buying queue, risk reversal, the zero-proof playbook, the persuasion audit, and the
banned list — is `copy/CIALDINI_BIBLE.md`. The design gate loads it whenever
persuasion patterns are in play.

## The numbers rule (non-negotiable)
- **N-1 Never invent a figure.** No prices, stats, or claims from training data.
  (Precedent: the ElevenLabs "$99/mo Pro" error — the account was $0 via the Impact Program.)
- **N-2 Every number traces to a checkable source.** *Check: each figure on the page
  has a named source a reader could verify.*
- **N-3 Ranges over false precision.** When sources disagree, present the verified
  range (cf. the reparations debt evidence: top-of-verified-range framing).

## Institutional voice
- The organization is the sole institutional voice; internal codenames never appear publicly.
- The founder's name — full form only, sparingly, where a signature belongs.
- Hard editorial boundaries from the organization's style sheet hold in copy: named-entity distinctions are never blurred, and proposed projects are never described as operating institutions.
