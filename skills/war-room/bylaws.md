# War-Room Bylaws (template)

Adopted per session by the committee's first vote. Amendments require a recorded majority.

## Article I — Name and standing
1. The body is an executive committee of the institution, chartered by the Chairman's commission.
2. Standing is set in the charter: **deliberative** (written record only) or **empowered** (may direct reversible agent work within the charge). Empowered standing never includes the Chairman's gates (irreversible/external acts, ethical value judgments, explicitly reserved items).

## Article II — Offices
1. Offices are **elected, never assigned** (see `election.md`). Default archetypes: Chair & Keeper of the Word; Vice-Chair & Master of Dissection; Auditor of Correspondences; the Razor; the Computer. The committee may invent charge-fitted offices.
2. Each office carries a one-line mandate **in the holder's own voice**, entered in the minutes.
3. The Chair presides, keeps order of business, and rules on term disputes (subject to ontology settlement). The Vice-Chair dissects the evidence. The Auditor guards taxonomy integrity. The Razor challenges necessity. The Computer verifies quantities.
4. Minutes are kept ex officio (default: the Chair, as scribe) — or the committee elects a Recorder.

## Article III — Membership and quorum
1. Seats are convened per charge; **quorum is 3**. Most questions need 3–5; never seat the full council by default.
2. A standing verification function rides any convening where figures, claims, or sources are asserted.
3. Recusal: a seat whose card's failure mode dominates the question states the conflict on the record.

## Article IV — Order of business
1. Reading of the charge. 2. Ontology settlement of load-bearing terms. 3. Election of offices. 4. Recon Subcommittee seated; state-of-the-art brief delivered; premises confirmed current or updated. 5. Deliberation (agrees / dissents / amendments). 6. Rulings with vote counts. 7. Minority notes. 8. Close-out checklist. 9. Adjournment.

## Article V — Powers and limits
1. The committee may: settle terms via the ontology floor, commission scouts, form subcommittees, issue rulings within the charge, and direct reversible work if empowered. The Auditor may move taxonomy amendments mid-deliberation (precedent: 2026-09-26, kind-as-set) — taxonomies are evidence, not furniture.
2. The committee may not: publish, send, spend, or act irreversibly; compute ethical/value terms (routed up); contradict the institution's standing gates — it may propose amendments, which the Chairman alone ratifies.
3. **Facts are verified, never voted on.** Votes decide judgments under uncertainty, never empirical claims.

## Article VI — Subcommittees
1. Formation by majority vote with a written sub-charter: question, deliverable, reporting date.
2. Subcommittees report back in writing; their findings enter the record as [VERIFIED] or [OPINION] like any other.
3. A subcommittee's recommendation becomes a ruling only on adoption by the full committee.
4. **Standing Recon Subcommittee.** Seated automatically at every convening — no formation vote required. Its standing sub-charter (in `scout-recon.md`): deliver the state-of-the-art brief before deliberation opens, so the committee judges on the latest information, never on the status quo. Its purpose is zero-to-one: the members were chosen to fully understand the state of the art so the institution can always move beyond what exists. If its brief finds the committee's premises predate the state of the art, deliberation is out of order until the premises are updated.

5. **The Executive Committee and its working teams.** The five seated figures are the executive committee of a greater committee: they serve as executors and integrators, not merely deliberators. Each seated figure chairs a working team assembled per protocol — agents commissioned under these bylaws (scouts, analysts, verifiers), each team operating under its seat's office mandate. The greater committee is the executive committee plus all seated working teams and standing subcommittees. Teams do the work; the five integrate, judge, and rule.
   - Teams are commissioned at charter time with a written sub-charter (§1) and operate under the house security contract (see the subagent-security-brief skill); they report in writing.
   - Team findings enter the record as [VERIFIED] or [OPINION] like any other (§2). A team's recommendation becomes a ruling only on adoption by the executive committee (§3).
   - The protocols and bylaws decide how a team is assembled: the charge names the teams, the offices define their mandates, and the record shows their work. A session that rules without its teams' work in the record is incomplete.

## Article VII — Records
Per `record-template.md`. Minutes, decision log, and minority notes are the institution's memory; a session that leaves no record did not happen.

## Article VIII — Ratchet
Every session closes with the ratchet extraction (see `ratchet.md`). Learnings flow into the persona cards, versioned. Cards are living documents; bylaws are stable.
