# Elections — offices are elected, never assigned

## Two procedures

**1. Acclamation** — permitted only when:
- every seated member's assent is **spoken aloud** (in-voice) and entered into the minutes verbatim,
- the tally is recorded (e.g., 5 of 5),
- no member requests a ballot.

Precedent (2026-09-26 formalism committee): Thoth proposed acclamation — "the voice is the oldest ballot"; Einstein seconded — "the simplest procedure that cannot fail is the right one"; Crowley consented on the ceremonial condition of spoken, recorded assent — "for what is not recorded did not happen"; Wolfram tallied. It worked because unanimity was genuine and the recording condition was honored.

**2. Ranked-choice ballot** — mandatory whenever acclamation fails, a seat requests it, or offices are contested. Instant runoff; publish the **rounds table** with the reason for each elimination (never silent tie-breaks). Majority (>50%) wins.

## Office-naming rule

Offices should be **charge-fitted**, not generic. "Chair" alone is weaker than "Chair & Keeper of the Word" when the charge is about naming. Each office takes a one-line mandate in the holder's own voice at election time. The rundown table is a required record element:

| Office | Holder | Mandate (in their own voice) |
|---|---|---|
| … | … | "…" |

## Rounds-table template

| Round | A (…) | B (…) | C (…) | Eliminated / Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | n (voters) | n (voters) | n (voters) | … |
| 2 | … | … | **n** (voters) | **X wins n–m** |

Tie-breaks state their reason in the record, never silently.

## Ratchet note

If acclamation is used, the minutes must show the spoken assents; a future audit that finds assent assumed rather than recorded voids the election. The convener's tally itself is checkable work — subject to the verification function like any other claim.
