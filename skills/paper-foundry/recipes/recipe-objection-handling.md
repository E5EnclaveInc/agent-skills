# Recipe: Objection Handling

Name the strongest objection early and answer it with the method, not with rhetoric. The exemplars' rule: the method is designed as the answer to the objection. If your answer to the strongest objection is a paragraph of prose, your design is incomplete — go back to recipe-contribution-claim.

## Step 1 — Name the single strongest objection

Write it in the objector's own voice, at full strength. The exemplars stage the objection before answering it:

- L1-E7: "surely there was a substantial difference [between value produced and subsistence]" — voiced, then left hanging while the numbers are promised.
- L1-E3 (full text): "more slaves were supplied by ethnic groups that initially had lower levels of trust" — reverse causality, stated in the introduction.
- L1-E3 (abstract): the "post-racial" climate and the behavioral-deficit narrative — named in the first two sentences.
- L2-E2: narrating beyond the archive repeats the archive's violence (invention as violation) — named inside the abstract's own terms.

Do not bundle three objections. One. The strongest. If you cannot decide which is strongest, ask which one a hostile reviewer would lead with.

## Step 2 — Answer it with the repertoire

**Preemption by staging** (L1-E7): let the reader hear the intuitive case at full strength, then promise the analysis that will discipline it. The refutation is the paper — but the reader must know the refutation is coming, or they will supply their own.

**Structural answer — design as rebuttal** (L1-E12): the within-Black comparison holds race constant and varies only enslavement history and Jim Crow exposure; the RD on location answers selection. The objection ("maybe it's something else about Black families") is never argued against — it is designed out. This is the strongest form of objection handling in the corpus: the objection cannot be restated after the design is understood.

**Falsification battery** (L1-E3, full text): after the IV answers the reverse-causality objection, falsification exercises (outside Africa; unexposed regions) answer the exclusion-restriction threat. Sequence: preempt → concede-the-hard-part ("far less clear") → answer → concede-the-boundary (short-run determinants still matter, with citations). The concessions are load-bearing: they show the author knows where the design ends.

**Concession-plus-redirect** (L1-E11): concede the legal difficulty (time has passed), then show the determination mechanism is political anyway — "political rather than narrowly legal processes will determine any ultimate claims." The objection is absorbed into a larger frame where it no longer defeats.

**Precedent-as-refutation** (L1-E11): the "too much time has passed" objection is disposed of by quantified existence proofs (French spoliation claims paid over 123 years; Haitian independence debt over 156 years). Numbers doing argumentative work as refutation.

**Sensitivity display** (L1-E9): the "your number depends on an arbitrary rate" attack is preempted by showing 3% and 6% compounding side by side. The sensitivity is the point; the range is the honest answer. Generalize: any parameter a reviewer could call arbitrary gets displayed, not hidden.

**Absorption** (L1-E1, L1-E8): the anticipated objection ("who pays / how much"; "courts can't do this") becomes a chapter or a design parameter rather than a counterargument. The legislative-vs-judicial clause concedes the institutional point and routes around it. Use when the objection is valid but not fatal.

**Displacement** (L1-E5): the objection (dysfunction explanations) is framed as the field's founding motivation and then left behind — the whole field is the refutation. Use only for field-level work; at paper level it reads as evasion.

**Confession** (L2-E2): the objection is conceded and converted into method — the writing "mimes the violence" of its attempts at redress. The vulnerability is the credential. Use for interpretive work where the objection (invention, overreach) cannot be designed out.

**Sidestep as deflection** (L1-E4): declare the contested question orthogonal — design questions are not downstream of the moral verdict. This works only if the paper genuinely delivers on the orthogonal dimension; otherwise it is avoidance.

**Universal-benefit close** (L1-E10): the zero-sum/particularism objection is answered in the closing move — the remedy benefits "the population at large." A classic of the reparations lane; place it at the end, where it reframes everything before it.

## Step 3 — Place the handling early

Where visible in the corpus, the strongest objection is named in the introduction (L1-E3, L2-E2, L1-E7) or the abstract (L1-E3's post-racial preemption). Objections handled in the conclusion read as afterthoughts. The one exception: the universal-benefit close (L1-E10), which is a reframing move, not a rebuttal — rebuttals go early, reframings go late.

## Check

- [ ] The strongest objection is stated in the objector's voice, at full strength, before the answer.
- [ ] The answer is in the method/design wherever possible, in prose only where the method cannot reach.
- [ ] Every concession is explicit and bounded (what is conceded, what is not).
- [ ] No straw men: the staged objection is one a real reviewer would actually raise.

Confidence notes: the structural and falsification moves are full-text grounded (L1-E3, L1-E12 via explicit abstract). Staging, absorption, and sidestep moves are abstract-legible. Confession (L2-E2) is abstract-visible but its extended mechanics are unverified against the text.
