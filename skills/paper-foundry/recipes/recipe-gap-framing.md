# Recipe: Gap Framing

Name the gap so the paper has a reason to exist. Run this before drafting anything else. Output: a one-sentence gap statement that names the consensus it displaces. If you cannot write that sentence, the paper is not ready to draft — do more reading, not more writing.

## Step 1 — Identify the consensus you will displace

Write down, in one clause, what the relevant readers currently believe. The exemplars never attack a vacuum; each names a specific incumbent. Pick the incumbent type that matches your paper:

- A quantitative consensus (L1-E1: the 10-cents-on-the-dollar wealth figure — displaced not by disputing it but by assembling what it implies).
- A public narrative (L1-E3: the "post-racial" story; L1-E6: the three-cornered shouting match over reparations).
- An impression (L1-E7: slave-trade profits were "fantastically" large — plausible on the surface, untested underneath).
- A disciplinary habit (L2-E2: the recovery project assumes the archive yields the enslaved subject; L2-E10: the Middle Passage told from the ship's books).
- A missing connection (L2-E14: Haiti and Cuba told as separate stories; the gap is a habit of separation, not a wrong claim).
- A causal arrow (L2-E7: Europe-centered accounts of the slave trade — reversed to Africa-shaped).
- An explanatory frame (L1-E5: group-based dysfunction explanations of inequality; L2-E13: "social death" as the master concept).

## Step 2 — Pick a gap move from the repertoire

Each move below is attested in the exemplars. Choose ONE. A paper with two gap moves is a paper with no gap.

1. **Comprehensiveness gap** (L1-E1): the evidence exists in pieces; nobody has assembled it. Made to matter by stakes escalation — each piece would suffice alone; together the case is impossible to ignore. Use when your contribution is synthesis at scale.
2. **Discursive/constraint gap** (L1-E3): the reigning narrative rules out the direct remedy, so the paper's contribution is a lawful workaround. Made to matter by showing the constraint is real — then designing within it. Use for policy-design papers under political constraints.
3. **Credit-and-vacate** (L1-E4): group prior work into a crowded bin (credited with "considerable attention") and an empty bin, then claim the empty bin. The gap needs no stakes argument — emptiness is the stakes. Use for taxonomy and design papers.
4. **Foundational-response gap** (L1-E5): the standard explanations are not merely incomplete but defamatory/wrong; the field exists as the response. High moral-methodological voltage. Use only if the existing frame is genuinely indefensible, not merely incomplete.
5. **Polemic-first, then empirical adjudication** (L1-E6): stage the fight (opponents' frames named and attributed), then let the data adjudicate instead of refuting anyone's morals. Use for opinion/political-economy papers.
6. **Revisionist steel-man** (L1-E7): state the received impression fairly — "plausible enough on the surface" — including its political load-bearing role, then discipline it with numbers. Never straw-man the target; the revision's authority comes from the fairness of the setup.
7. **Whether-to-how** (L1-E8): the debate is stuck on *whether*; move to *how*. Open from shared definitional ground (a comparative framework), then particularize. The missing pieces are operational: magnitude, eligibility, institutional route.
8. **Benchmark-verdict gap** (L1-E9): stipulate one anchor number, then set the literature's estimates against it. The gap is the missing comparison; the paper's verdict function is "does it cover the target, and by how much."
9. **Two-problem framing** (L1-E11): name the problems cleanly in parallel (legal + economic; obstacle + estimation), then show they are *old* problems with precedents. The upward correction comes from better method, not new problems.
10. **Within-group gap** (L1-E12): the literature studies the gap *between* groups; study the gap *inside* the group (enslaved-until-1865 vs. free-before-1865). A gap inside the gap isolates the mechanism and preempts selection objections structurally.
11. **Paradox opening** (L2-E2, L2-E14, L2-E13, L2-E10): stage a contradiction in the first breath — ubiquity plus unknowability; revolution next door to entrenchment; the concept vs. the scene it cannot see. The contradiction *is* the gap. (The history lane's signature move; see recipe-intro-conclusion.)
12. **Archive-as-method gap** (L2-E2, L2-E6): the source base's hostility or silence is not a limitation — it is the finding. Convert the archive's bias into the method (critical fabulation; reading along the bias). Use when the evidence base is fragmentary by structure.
13. **Separation gap** (L2-E14): two stories usually told separately, told together. Non-adversarial: no predecessor is attacked; the missing connection is the contribution.
14. **Inversion gap** (L2-E7): reverse the causal arrow. Temporal or geographic bounding ("before 1680") delimits the claim so the inversion doesn't overreach.

## Step 3 — Make the gap matter

A gap without stakes is a footnote. The exemplars make gaps matter by one of: stakes escalation (each piece suffices; together undeniable — L1-E1); constraint-routing (the gap rules out the direct fix — L1-E3); implication (proposals can't become programs without design — L1-E4); verdict function (the comparison decides the policy — L1-E9); canon indictment (the omission indicts the canon — L2-E9). Pick one and write it as the second half of your gap sentence.

## Step 4 — Check

- [ ] The gap sentence names the displaced consensus explicitly.
- [ ] Exactly one gap move is used.
- [ ] The stakes clause is present and is not "this is understudied."
- [ ] The gap is falsifiable-adjacent: a reader can tell what would close it.

Confidence notes: gap-framing is the most abstract-legible move in the corpus — all moves above are grounded in verified abstract or description text, except move 12's full-text mechanics (L2-E2's "mimes the violence" confession is abstract-visible; the extended fabulation method is not) and move 14's internal structure (L2-E7's inversion is abstract-visible; its comparative-institutional machinery is not).
