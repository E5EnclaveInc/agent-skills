# Recipe: Literature-Review Positioning

Make room for the contribution: credit every predecessor by name, group them, delimit them, and state exactly the room your paper occupies. Rule: no straw men. A predecessor described unfairly is a reviewer you have manufactured.

## Step 1 — Group the prior work

The exemplars never list predecessors one by one; they sort them into bins, and the bins do the positioning work:

- **Two bins, one empty** (L1-E4): moral/political arguments (crowded, "considerable attention") vs. design analysis (empty). Credit the crowded bin; claim the empty one.
- **Three credited piles plus the slot** (L1-E3, full text): (a) why trust matters economically, (b) empirical culture literature, (c) historical determinants of culture — and the paper is "the natural next step" after (a)+(b), "like the recent contributions" of (c). Generous credit, precise delimitation: the room made is exactly one mechanism wide.
- **Method-lineage** (L1-E9): Darity's, Marketti's, and Craemer's estimation traditions named as three schools; the paper positions itself as synthesizer and extender. Prior estimators credited by name, their numbers reused — cumulative, not adversarial.
- **Precedent class** (L1-E11): historical comparators (French spoliation, Haitian debt) grouped as a class of multigenerational payment programs, credited as existence proofs. The existing estimates positioned as the baseline to be corrected upward — credited for existing, delimited by method.
- **Ecumenical genealogy** (L1-E5): nine named ancestors across economics, sociology, psychology. The field presented as convergence of established names, not a break. The one named outsider (group-based dysfunction) is the position the field defines itself against.
- **Reclassification** (L1-E13): the entire prior estimation literature re-sorted as instances of two strategies (itemization vs. global indicator) — subordinating previous estimators, including the authors' own earlier work, to the new taxonomy. The mature form: the literature reviewing itself.

## Step 2 — Choose the positioning stance

- **Credit-and-vacate** (L1-E4): praise the existing conversation, then claim the room it isn't using. Lowest-conflict stance; requires the empty room to genuinely exist.
- **Affiliation-plus-extension** (L2-E4): same lineage (Hartman/Spillers), new method ("wake work"). Claim the mantle; extend it. The lineage is the credential.
- **Inversion** (L2-E7): the existing literature's causal arrow reversed (Europe-centered → Africa-shaped). Delimit temporally or geographically so the inversion doesn't overreach.
- **Connection** (L2-E14): the field's habit of telling stories separately named as the problem; the book as synthesis. Non-adversarial — no predecessor attacked; the missing connection is the contribution.
- **Displacement by design** (L1-E5): the objectionable frame named as the field's founding motivation, then left behind. Paper-level use reads as evasion; reserve for field-level claims.
- **Against positions, not studies** (L1-E6): position against ideological camps (greed-motive, divisiveness) rather than named papers; the empirical results adjudicate the camps. Works for political-economy papers where the "literature" is a public fight.

## Step 3 — Delimit without distorting

Every grouping above includes a delimitation clause — the sentence that says what the predecessors do *not* cover, which is where your paper lives:

- L1-E4: design analysis "received almost none" of the attention.
- L1-E3: prior work "unable to pin down the exact causal mechanisms."
- L1-E7: historians' "impressions" — unnamed, collective, characterized as impressionistic (use this characterization only if it is true; the paper's authority depends on the fairness of the setup — see recipe-gap-framing, revisionist steel-man).
- L2-E11: prior literature described the 19th century and smuggled it backward — delimited temporally ("before cotton was king").

Write your delimitation as one sentence. If it requires adjectives like "surprisingly" or "shockingly," it is probably overstated.

## Step 4 — Check the venue's positioning dialect

- Economics journals: positioning is by *mechanism slot* — the paper is the next mechanism, the better identification, the cleaner design (L1-E3, L1-E12). Predecessors are slotted, not argued with.
- History journals/monographs: positioning is by *reframing* — inversion, connection, affiliation-extension (L2-E7, L2-E14, L2-E4). Direct attacks on named predecessors are rare in the extractable sample; the room is made by re-framing, not refuting.
- Field journals (RBPE, Du Bois Review): positioning may be interventionist — against public narratives and debate stages (L1-E3, L1-E6, L1-E8), not just against scholarship.

## Check

- [ ] Every predecessor that matters is named and credited for what it actually established.
- [ ] The delimitation sentence is verifiable — a reader can check whether the claimed gap exists.
- [ ] No predecessor is a straw man; the strongest version of each prior position is the one delimited.
- [ ] The stance matches the venue's dialect.

Confidence notes: grouping moves are well-grounded (L1-E3 full text; L1-E4, L1-E9, L1-E11, L1-E13 abstract-legible). History-lane positioning (inversion, connection, affiliation) rests on abstracts and publisher descriptions — the moves are visible, their extended execution is not.
