# Recipe: Evidence Weave

Set evidence-to-claim density: how close each claim sits to its evidence, by genre. Rule: no substantive claim floats more than one paragraph from the evidence that carries it. A claim that needs three pages of setup before its evidence appears is a claim the reader has already stopped believing.

## Step 1 — Choose the density norm for your genre

**Economics-empirical:** every major claim is a number with its method and rate attached *in the same sentence* (L1-E9: "$18.6T at 3% to $6.2 quadrillion at 6%" — the argument is the table of estimates). Each causal claim names its dataset and specification in the same paragraph (L1-E3, full text: Afrobarometer 2005 merged to Nunn-2008 slave-export estimates via Murdock-1959 ethnic matching). No claim floats free of its test. If your paragraph states a finding without the data source and specification, it fails this norm.

**Economics-design/argument:** the gap is stipulated as background ("the enormous and persistent black-white wealth gap" — L1-E3); the evidence burden is carried by the mechanism design's logic, with each claim sitting adjacent to its *design rationale* rather than to data. Adjacency still holds — claim, then rationale, in the same breath — but the rationale is a design argument, not a dataset.

**History-interpretive:** evidence sits *inside* the claim. The cold-open vignette (L2-E13: named ship, year, witness, quoted phrases, spatial arrangement — the macro-conceptual claim arrives embedded in the micro-scene). The emblematic figure (L2-E2: Venus is both the evidence and the argument; "convergence of terror and pleasure" is read directly off her recurrence). The paired case (L2-E14: Haitian events and Cuban responses held in the same frame). The evidence is not presented *then* interpreted — it performs the interpretation.

**History-structural:** claims operate at the level of institutions, polities, and systems (L2-E7: African military capacity, estate structure, demography); evidence is comparative-institutional. Density is medium by design — structures cannot be evidenced in a single scene — but each structural claim still names the institutional comparison that carries it.

**Survey/field-review:** the cited lineage *is* the evidence (L1-E5: the field's existence is evidenced by its nine named predecessors). Maximal density between claim ("the field draws on X") and evidence (X's work). Every name earns its place; a name without a stated intellectual debt is decoration.

## Step 2 — Use the compression devices

- **Promissory accounting identity** (L1-E7): display the logic of the evidence up front (value produced minus subsistence consumption) so the reader can audit the forthcoming numbers. Claim and method adjacent; claim and number may be separated (numbers live in the tables) — but the identity must be stated before the first number lands.
- **Dual-use anchor** (L1-E8): one device that is both evidence and method (the 1865 land promise: a historical fact *and* a valuation basis). Compresses density; do not explain it twice.
- **Enumeration-then-abstraction** (L1-E4): list the case inventory (internment, Holocaust, lesser-known programs), then induce the design options directly on top of the list. The abstraction never travels far from the enumeration.
- **Inventory-then-judgment** (L1-E13): list the itemized components, list the global indicators, then the verdict sits directly on the comparison. No new estimate is produced — the density is in the completeness of the inventory.
- **Convergence display** (L1-E9): independent methods, same region, one paragraph per method, numbers attached. The convergence is visible on the page, not asserted in prose.
- **Associative multi-archive** (L2-E4): literary, visual, cinematic, quotidian sources composed together — "refusing to distinguish between artistic and scholarly sources." Density is associative rather than tabular; the composition is part of the argument. Use only when the thesis is about cultural meaning, not causal magnitude.

## Step 3 — Mark evidentiary status honestly

Where the contribution is a modeled world, keep the rungs legible: observed vs. back-calculated vs. simulated (L1-E10). Where the method is counterfactual, say "would have." Where categories are unfinished, name them (L1-E9: lost opportunities, pain and suffering flagged as future work — the concession protects the published estimates from over-claim charges). Where the archive is fragmentary by structure, say so and make the fragmentariness the method (L2-E6: the archive's hostility *is* the finding).

## Check

- [ ] Every substantive claim has its evidence in the same paragraph (empirical) or performs its evidence (interpretive).
- [ ] No table of numbers appears before the accounting identity that makes it auditable.
- [ ] Counterfactual rungs are labeled; unfinished categories are named, not hidden.
- [ ] Modular blocks (L1-E1): if one estimate collapses, does the argument survive? If not, add redundancy.

Confidence notes: the empirical density norms are the best-grounded in the corpus (L1-E3 full text; L1-E9's unusually complete structured abstract). The interpretive norms rest on one article opening (L2-E13), abstracts (L2-E2), and publisher descriptions (L2-E4, L2-E10) — the vignette mechanics are verified; the book-length weaving is inferred.
