# Demand Mining

## Purpose
Find problems people will pay to have solved — with receipts — and rank them
before a single line of product is written.

## Workflow
1. Set the hunting ground: which public sources (social replies, forums,
   reviews, inboxes you own) and the one question they answer ("what should
   agents do for you?").
2. Mine raw problem statements. Keep the quotes and the links — never
   paraphrase the pain away.
3. Cluster into themes. Kill every theme with no identifiable buyer, in
   writing.
4. Score the survivors on two axes: willingness-to-pay (evidence of spend,
   cost complaints, or hiring for the job — never inferred from enthusiasm)
   × speed-to-launch (days, not quarters).
5. For the top themes, fill the buyer card: buyer (one-line ICP), contact
   path (DM, email, landing signup), offer shape, price band, and Day-1
   interview questions.
6. Flag feasibility risks per theme (licensing, data access, trust, platform
   rules) before any build starts.
7. Force the thesis: one paragraph — customer, painful problem, wedge product,
   why now. Kill the rest with one line each; nothing lingers.

## Output Contract
A ranked theme table (theme, buyer, WTP evidence, speed, contact path, price
band, feasibility risks), the one-paragraph thesis, and the killed list.

## Operating Rules
1. No theme survives without an identifiable buyer AND a contact path.
2. Willingness-to-pay needs evidence. Enthusiasm is not evidence.
3. Rank before building. The ranking is the deliverable, not the research dump.
4. Raw quotes with links beat summaries. Keep the receipts.
5. One thesis wins; the others are killed in writing, not left to linger.
6. Demand signals go stale — date every quote, re-mine on a cadence.
